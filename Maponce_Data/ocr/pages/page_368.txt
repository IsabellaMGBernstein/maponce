328 CARTAS SOBRE LA REVOLUCIÓN
164. Carta a Yamagata
Nuevo plan del Ejército Filipino.—Revolución en Negros y en
lloilo.—Libre entrada á barcos mercantes extranjeros
637 Miyokoji Y ama, Kitagata, Korakigun,
Yokohama, April 11th, 1899.
Mr. Y. Yamacara, Esq.
“The Yorodzu Choho,” Tokyo.

Dear Sir: 1 have received informations dated at Malolos
the 18th of last month. According to them we must not be
astonished that Malolos may be taken by Americans. 1 do -
not know, however, if that place has already been taken. The
new plan of our army, in order to avoid too much blood to
be poured, ís to let Americans take many places, and attack
them in guerrillas at several posts. When the Americans
are scattered it will be easy to destroy them with lítele blood.
This proceeding has always given result: my people is very
skilful in this kind of struggle. They only accept advantageous
battles. The sun at depopulated places and fields will help
us. Íam also reported that in Negros Island the Americans
have found a great revolution. The American transport
Indiana convoyed there, lately, about 1,500 men from Manila
to succour their people. In lloilo, the same thing happens.
They cannot go further, always hostilized by our troops. My
Government knows that the independence will be as better
loved, if the sacrifice is greater, and for this reason ít does not
lose patience, hoping always for an early triumph. They
will not yield without the independence of the Archipelago.
I am also reported that our Government, ín a decree dated the
12th of March, commands that all foreign trade steamers be
admitted, free of duties, in all our ports both for selling and
buying merchandise.

Wishing you good health, 1 remain, dear Sir,

Yours very truly,
M. Ponce.
