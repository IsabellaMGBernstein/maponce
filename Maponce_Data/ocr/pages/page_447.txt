MARIANO PONCE, 407
208. Carta a Sun Y at Sen
Llega a Hongkong.—¿Cómo están nuestros asuntos Dr. Sun Yat
Sen?—Bastan dos millones y medio de balas.—El remanente del
dinero para compra de rífles.—No haya demoras.—A la vuelta
del barco habrá recursos.—Información del desastre, y so-
licitud de socorro para los damnificados, ya enviadas
Dr. Sun Yar Sen. Oct. 16, 1899.
My Dear FrienD:

I inform you of our happy arrival here.

ÍI suppose Mr. Pak must be already with you there, because
he was not here on my arrival. Please give him my best
compliments.

How is the state of our affairs? At present it will be enough
che two millions and half we bought. All excess of money,
after buying the vessel, 1 beg you to employ it in rifles belonging
to the same cartridges, never mind whether a hundred, or
more or less, all number the money can, if this will not delay
che departure. The matter now for us ís to avoid all delay,
but if you can include a few number of rifles, without this
inconvenience, ít will be better.

Í regret that we cannot dispose of more money that we have
at hand. But, 1 hope that, on return, the vessel will bring to
us money, because our people are waiting now in the place
you know, with money destinated . . . .

I have sent already to our Government full information and
application in favour of the damnified persons in the last disaster.
I hope we shall have satisfactory resolution.

Please give my best and hearty compliments to Messts.
Nakamura, Inukaí and all our friends there.

Í remain, dear sir,
Yours truly,
M. Poncr.*
Dx. Sun Yar Sen. * Hongkong, 16 de Octubre de 1899.
Mi Querimo AmiGO:

Informo á V. de nuestro feliz arribo aquí.

Supongo que Mr. Pak ya debe de estar allí 4 su lado, pues no le encontré
aquí á mi llegada. Sírvase darle mis afectuosas expresiones.
